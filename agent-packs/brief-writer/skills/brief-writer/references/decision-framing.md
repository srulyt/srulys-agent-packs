# Decision Framing

Use this guidance for the optional decision sections: options, metrics, plan, and financials. Include a section only when the source supports it. See `../assets/brief-template.md` for the table shapes.

## Lead with the Recommendation

1. **Recommendation first.** "[Option] because [one-sentence business-outcome rationale]." Write the rationale so a non-expert can repeat it confidently.
2. **Alternatives.** Give each one a short tradeoff statement in business terms.
3. **Comparison table.** Put all options side by side against the criteria, including a stakeholder-friendly impact column.

Do not make the reader parse every option before learning the recommendation.

In a settled-design brief the choice is already made. Present the options as execution tradeoffs, not as an open decision.

## Options and Tradeoffs

- Include at least two viable options, including the status quo when relevant.
- Use explicit criteria: impact, cost, time, risk, reversibility, and strategic fit.
- Say why the recommended option wins in this context.

## Success Metrics

Use 3–7 high-signal KPIs or OKRs. Give each one:

- a baseline, if known
- a target
- a guardrail
- a measurement method and cadence

## Plan, Milestones, Dependencies

- Phase the work: crawl, walk, run, or an equivalent.
- Identify the key milestones and the risky dependencies.
- Name owners when known. Otherwise mark the owner as unknown rather than inventing one.

## Financials and Resourcing

- **Cost drivers:** people, time, infrastructure, vendors.
- **Value drivers:** revenue, savings, risk reduction, strategic enablement.
- Use ranges under uncertainty and avoid false precision.
- State the assumptions behind every estimate.

## Closing Readiness

| Closing | Ready when |
|---|---|
| Decision Ask | The scope and commitment are explicit, the timing is clear, and leadership can understand the metrics and risk posture. |
| Recommendation | The direction is clearly stated, the rationale is backed by evidence, and the impact of adopting it vs not is concrete. |
| Next Steps | The actions are specific and ordered, owners are named or flagged as unknown, and the timeline is stated. |
| Call to Action | The ask is specific (what, from whom, by when), the reason is clear, and the step after the input arrives is stated. |
| Summary | The takeaways are synthesized rather than restated, the current state is clear, and the conditions for future action are identified. |
